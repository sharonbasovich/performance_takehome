### Classification rules

- **As-of:** a quote is usable only if `observed_at ≤ as-of < valid_until`. Observations after the cutoff are look-ahead and never enter the primary view; expired quotes are historical context only.
- **Product tier:** hardware, memory, region, isolation, preemption and SLA (≥ reference) must match exactly; otherwise the quote is a different product (cross-check at most).
- **Contract class:** `reserved_capacity` needs guaranteed capacity, a stated delivery window and no cancellation — the reference contract. Cancel-anytime hourly usage is `flexible_on_demand` (it embeds a cancellation option), a spend commitment without a window or guarantee is `usage_commitment` (a discount on current usage, **not** a forward), and preemptible capacity is `spot`. Remaining unsupported rights are `unclassified_rights`; a kind label alone does not prove cancellation rights.
- Only valid + comparable + reserved-capacity quotes become curve nodes.

### Normalization ladder

currency → unit (`gpu_hour` as is; `node_hour` ÷ GPUs; `node_month` ÷ GPUs ÷ actual hours of the stated month) → fees (none evidenced in the demo; optional columns supported) → payment timing (advance payments compounded to the month-end arrears convention at the buyer's discount rate over the billed window) → quantity (node-sized quotes expressed as single-GPU equivalents).

### Method

1. **Observed nodes.** A two-sided tenor uses the mid as the point and the bid–ask as the interval. A one-sided tenor keeps the quoted side as one interval edge and estimates the other with max(15% of the quote, widest observed two-sided spread = 0.5000). A one-sided quote is an interval edge, not a mid; the anchor table shows which sides are actually present in this case.
2. **Implied nodes.** Linear interpolation between bracketing anchors, or anchors built only from quantity-adjusted / strip quotes (the classification follows the evidence in the anchor table).
3. **Modeled / forecast nodes.** Beyond the last anchor the mid follows `F(t) = A + (F_L − A)·exp(−κ·(t − t_L))` toward the base anchor A = full-cost breakeven 2.9878 (supply-cost view below). κ = 0.1010/month, fitted-least-squares-log-deviation on observed forward tenors [1, 3]. Nodes within 6 months of the last anchor are MODELED; further out they are FORECAST (judgmental).
4. **Bands.** The interval beyond the last anchor is the union over the three scenario anchors and κ ∈ [0.0, 0.3] (so it always contains the flat extension of the last anchor interval and the fast reversion to each anchor), plus a random-walk band of 0.08·√(months)·(anchor high). The band depends only on anchor intervals and declared parameters, never on the fitted κ. The demo's individual forward-quote removals widen these bands, but this is **not a universal removal invariant**: removing quantity-pair evidence or the last anchor can change the price scale, scenarios, and node identification. Widths can then narrow despite weaker evidence; these judgmental scenario ranges are not statistical confidence intervals. A node-sized quote with no supported quantity translation is excluded rather than promoted to direct evidence.
5. **Labels.** OBSERVED / IMPLIED / MODELED / FORECAST / UNIDENTIFIED are carried into `curve.csv` from the current evidence; no tenor is assumed observed solely because it was requested.

### Scarcity anchor: choice and bias

The scarcity scenario reverts toward 5.2000, the highest valid same-hardware ask at as-of across *all* contract classes — in this pack the cancel-anytime single-GPU on-demand ask. That price embeds a cancellation option and single-GPU granularity, so as an anchor for non-cancellable reserved capacity it is biased **upward**: the scenario is deliberately a stress ('forwards re-price to today's dearest flexible access'), not a central estimate. Reserved-class evidence alone would give a lower anchor (the highest reserved ask) and a milder stress; the choice is a declared config parameter (`anchors.scarcity.kind`) and can be switched without code changes.

### Why the current on-demand asks are context, not the 1-month node

The reference contract is non-cancellable reserved capacity. Cancel-anytime hourly asks (`q01` 5.20 per GPU-hour on 1 GPU(s), `q02` 4.50 per GPU-hour on 8 GPU(s)) embed a cancellation option and do not guarantee capacity in a future month, so they bound a one-month reservation from above rather than price it. The paired current-month evidence puts the flexibility premium at 1.111× (`q02` 4.5000 vs reserved `q07` 4.0490 arrears-equivalent). The firm 2026-11 ask 4.9000 anchors the first node as its upper edge; the on-demand asks above are the context it is checked against.

### Market view versus supply-cost view

The observed market nodes (2026-11 4.5325, 2027-01 4.2500) sit well above the full-cost breakeven 2.9878 at the operator's declared 65% utilization, and above even the breakeven of a fully utilized reserved GPU (2.0855). The near curve slopes down toward cost, which is why the model reverts toward it rather than extrapolating the slope.

**Why they disagree, and why cost is neither a floor nor a cap.** Capacity is not storable, so there is no cash-and-carry arbitrage tying forward prices to cost. In oversupply the relevant floor is the short-run marginal cost (0.4100): capex is sunk and an operator keeps renting below full cost rather than idle. In scarcity prices exceed full cost for as long as new supply (lead times, power, financing) takes to arrive. Full cost is only where entry and exit decisions balance over the economic life — a long-run attractor, so we use it as a soft anchor with a fitted reversion speed and explicit bands, not as a bound.

### Decision and when it flips

**do_not_sign_at_this_price** — mark negative at mid; the interval includes zero.

- sign at or below the fair fixed price 4.1333 USD/GPU-hour (mid curve); interval 3.5503..4.7861
- base scenario fair fixed price 4.1333 -> contract at 4.50 is unfavourable to the buyer
- cheap_compute scenario fair fixed price 3.8231 -> contract at 4.50 is unfavourable to the buyer
- scarcity scenario fair fixed price 4.5647 -> contract at 4.50 is favourable to the buyer
- Default risk makes an out-of-the-money contract *less* negative for the buyer under zero recovery (release from an above-market fee); the sensitivity table shows the effect for this case.

- The contract rate $4.5000 is below the comparable current flexible ask at the reference quantity $5.2000/GPU-hour. That as-of quote does not guarantee future availability or future prices, and flexible access retains a cancellation option.

Valuation assumptions:

- discount rate 0.0500 annual-effective, ACT/365, from as-of 2026-10-01T00:00:00Z to each month-end payment date
- SLA 99.9%: provider assumed to deliver exactly at the floor; unavailable hours earn a pro-rata fee credit and no consequential damages, so value scales by availability
- provider default probability 0.0200 over the deal spread uniformly from as-of to delivery end; recovery 0.00 applies only to a positive buyer claim; undelivered months are neither paid nor received
- cancellation none: no optionality value for either side is added
- egress and taxes excluded from the capacity price on both the contract and the curve

**Evidence that would reduce uncertainty.** Firm two-sided quotes for requested delivery months not directly observed (2027-04, 2027-10), and for modeled reservation months; a second provider's reserved quote at the same quantity; archived bid/ask time series to estimate κ and the band volatility instead of declaring them; the provider's credit profile; and actual SLA attainment history.

**Falsifiable checks.** New firm quotes outside a modeled interval challenge its scenario assumptions; if firm long-tenor quotes print below the full-utilization breakeven, the cheap scenario is too mild; if cancellable on-demand prints below reserved capacity at the same quantity, the flexibility-premium reading is wrong.

## Limitations

- Synthetic inputs: no provenance, licences, or real timestamps; the output is a plumbing demonstration.
- The one-sided spread fraction, reversion band, horizon volatility and scenario anchors are declared parameters (`research/config/default.json`), informed by but not estimated from a time series.
- κ source: fitted-least-squares-log-deviation; observed forward tenors [1, 3]. Sparse observations limit identification.
- The quantity factor pairs quotes from different providers, so it carries provider effects.
- The H200 translation uses one synthetic throughput measurement and ignores memory, software and demand mix.
- Default treatment assumes uniform default timing (linear cumulative probability), not a constant hazard, and release of both sides on default; operational disruption costs beyond fee credits are not quantified. SLA attainment is assumed at the floor.
- Energy is charged per allocated hour at full draw; idle draw and the case's 'includes_energy' nuance for quoted prices are not modeled separately.
- One-sided anchor tenors [0, 1] have estimated opposite sides; requested delivery months not directly observed are ['2027-04', '2027-10']. The decision and interval must be read from this case's valuation, not a fixed demo conclusion.

## Issues found in supplied infrastructure
